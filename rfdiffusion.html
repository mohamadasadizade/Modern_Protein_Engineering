COURSE / 03 RFDIFFUSION
PART 03 · GENERATIVE PROTEIN DESIGN

De novo protein
binder design

A guided workflow covering target-interface concepts, diffusion-based backbone generation, sequence design and structural evaluation.

1. Protein-binding sites and interface residues

Protein–protein binding interfaces are formed by spatially organised residues from interacting molecular partners. The workshop introduces interface geometry, target accessibility, residue numbering and the idea of selecting hotspot residues to guide conditional design.

Hotspot residues

Hotspots are selected target residues used to focus or condition the design task. Their choice should be supported by structural context and biological knowledge; a hotspot list alone does not guarantee a functional binder.

2. How diffusion models generate protein backbones

Diffusion-based generative models learn to reverse a corruption process. During generation, the model iteratively refines a noisy representation toward a plausible protein backbone while incorporating the specified conditioning information.

Target structure + design constraints
↓
Initial noisy / partial representation
↓
Iterative denoising by RFdiffusion
↓
Candidate binder backbone

For conditional binder design, the target structure and selected interface constraints guide generation. The resulting backbone is a candidate geometry, not evidence of experimentally confirmed binding.

3. Installation and computational setup

The practical environment for this workshop uses Docker to package the software and its dependencies. The host NVIDIA driver provides access to the GPU; the container supplies the Linux runtime and model environment required by the selected image.

4. Conditional binder design: practical workflow

  1. Prepare the target structure and confirm chain/residue numbering.
  2. Choose the target region and define the conditioning constraints.
  3. Configure binder length and generation settings.
  4. Generate candidate backbones with RFdiffusion.
  5. Inspect outputs and retain candidates for downstream sequence design.

Demonstration target: insulin receptor structure PDB 4ZXB (the exact chain, crop and residue mapping must be recorded in the final protocol).

5. Sequence design with ProteinMPNN

ProteinMPNN is used after backbone generation to propose amino-acid sequences compatible with a supplied protein structure. Sequence design is a distinct stage from backbone generation. Candidate sequences should be tracked alongside the corresponding backbone identifiers and design parameters.

6. Structural validation and result analysis

Candidate designs should be filtered and examined using a combination of structural and sequence-based criteria. Depending on the available tools, this may include structure prediction, interface inspection, confidence assessment, geometry checks and comparison across candidate designs.

Backbone quality

Inspect chain continuity, clashes, geometry and fold plausibility.

Sequence plausibility

Review sequence diversity and compatibility with the designed backbone.

Interface analysis

Inspect contacts, buried surface and interface geometry.

Independent validation

Use appropriate prediction and assessment tools; computational agreement is not experimental confirmation.

Important

A successful computational design pipeline produces candidates for further evaluation. Experimental expression and binding assays are required to establish whether a designed binder actually binds its target.
