PART 01 · CONCEPTUAL BACKGROUND

Foundations of
computational protein design

The concepts needed to understand modern machine-learning methods for protein structure prediction and design.

1. Introduction to computational protein modelling and design

Computational protein modelling aims to infer structural or functional properties of proteins from available information, especially their amino-acid sequences. Computational protein design works in the opposite direction: it seeks sequences or structures with desired properties.

Modelling

Given a sequence or other evidence, estimate a plausible structure or property.

Design

Specify a desired structural or functional goal and generate candidate proteins.

2. Structure-to-sequence and sequence-to-structure approaches

Protein modelling and design can be framed as two complementary directions. In sequence-to-structure tasks, a sequence is used to predict a compatible three-dimensional structure. In structure-to-sequence tasks, a structural scaffold or backbone is used to identify amino-acid sequences that may fold into that structure.

3. Applications of deep neural networks

Deep neural networks learn complex statistical relationships from large datasets. In structural biology, they can be used for tasks such as structure prediction, sequence representation, contact or interaction prediction, sequence design and generative modelling.

Key idea

A neural network learns a mapping from input representations to outputs by adjusting its parameters to reduce a training objective. The learned mapping is useful only to the extent that the training data, model assumptions and evaluation procedure support the intended task.

4. Transformer architectures and protein language models

Transformers use attention mechanisms to model relationships between positions in a sequence. Protein language models apply related ideas to amino-acid sequences, learning contextual representations from large collections of protein sequences.

These representations can support downstream tasks, including classification, property prediction and structure-related modelling. Attention is a mechanism for contextual information exchange; it should not automatically be interpreted as a direct biological explanation.

5. Diffusion models in generative protein design

Diffusion models learn to generate data by progressively denoising a perturbed representation. In protein design, diffusion-based approaches can generate candidate protein backbones under specified constraints or conditioning information.

For binder design, conditioning can include a target structure and selected interface residues. The generated backbone is a structural proposal; sequence design and downstream structural evaluation are separate steps.

Transition to the practical modules

AlphaFold is introduced as a structure-prediction system. RFdiffusion is introduced as a generative design approach. They address different tasks and should not be treated as interchangeable tools.
